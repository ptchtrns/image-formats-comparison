Notes

  • Why no PNG column? PNG is a lossless format: it has no quality setting, only compression effort (which changes encode time, not the pixels). Its output would always be pixel-identical to the input, so it is not comparable with the lossy results above and is left out.
  • What the quality numbers mean. Each value is passed straight to the encoder on a 0 – 100 scale, where 100 is the least compression and 10 the most. The number is not a percentage of visual fidelity, and it is not comparable across formats:
    • JPEG (mozjpeg): the classic libjpeg scale. It scales the DCT quantisation tables; 100 keeps them at 1 (near-lossless), 50 uses the reference tables, lower values coarsen them further. At 90 and above chroma is kept at full resolution (4:4:4); below 90 the encoder switches to 4:2:0 subsampling, so 90 → 70 is a bigger visual step than the number suggests. Encoding uses mozjpeg's recommended settings (progressive, optimised Huffman coding, its own quant table), not plain libjpeg defaults.
    • WebP (libwebp): controls quantiser strength and filtering. Roughly, WebP at a given number looks like JPEG at a somewhat higher number, in a smaller file.
    • AVIF (libavif + libaom): libavif maps it linearly onto the AV1 quantiser (0 – 63, inverted). AVIF holds up much better at low numbers than JPEG or WebP, so AVIF 40 can be closer to JPEG 70 in appearance.
    Compare formats by looking at the image at a given file size, not at the same quality number.
  • What SSIM means here. Each result is decoded again and compared with the original: mean SSIM over non-overlapping 8×8 luma blocks. 1.000 is identical; roughly, ≥ 0.95 is hard to tell apart at 1:1, ≤ 0.90 is visibly degraded. It is a simplified SSIM (no Gaussian window, luma only), fine for ranking encodes of the same image, not a substitute for looking. The "estimated size at SSIM 0.95" line is the fair cross-format comparison: equal measured quality, different sizes. The size is interpolated between the two quality steps that bracket 0.95, so it is an estimate, not an actual file.
  • Crops. Each cell shows the centre 200×200 pixels of the decoded result at 1:1, so artefacts are visible rather than inferred from a number. Zoom the page if the crops are too small.
